## Review of the arguments

**Geometry and dynamics (lines 1237–1651).** I checked the frame recursion, disjoint-zone decomposition, cross-product estimates, determinant area formula, diameter and perimeter bounds, and norm growth in Lemma `lem:geom`. The induction for the inverse-matrix estimate uses row cross products and correctly counts each cofactor column twice. The derivative calculation in Proposition `lem: Dpsi` gives strict decrease of distances away from the parabolic vertex. The proof of Theorem `cor:singlenode` obtains a uniform Hausdorff convergence estimate from cylinder diameters, and its exclusion of chain recurrence outside the gasket correctly treats short return chains by repetition. The accumulation and freeness arguments follow from zone disjointness. I found no substantive gap here.

**Affinity exponent and cut-out geometry (lines 1653–1926).** I checked the singular-value bounds and the comparisons of zone diameter and center derivative with sigma_2/sigma_1. In particular, the restriction estimate for the cofactor map in Lemma `lem:DI` uses a right singular vector and controls its projection onto the tangent plane. Proposition `prop:phiD` correctly converts the two-dimensional singular-value function into a norm factor and a directional derivative factor. The collar estimate then gives the upper box-dimension bound, and the asserted external equality of Hausdorff dimension with affinity exponent yields the existence and equality of box dimension.

**Run operator and finite certificates (lines 1928–2437).** The exact norm and derivative products along runs agree with the order of matrix composition. The trapping estimate supplies summability of the run weights for the asserted parameter range. The positive-test-function lemma and the decomposition of the full series into an initial run followed by iterates justify both directions of Proposition `cor:certA`. I checked the permutation reduction, the directional coordinate formulas, the interpolation estimates on boxes and triangular prisms, and the signs in Proposition `prop:P1`. The use of almost-everywhere Hessians in Lemma `lem:interp` is explicitly justified by a Fubini argument and continuity. The convex-function sum bounds at lines 2311–2314 have the correct directions. The mathematical scheme for grouped tails, increment bounds, and directed rounding is consistent as described. Whether the implementation fulfills all these descriptions remains outside this text-only review.

**General cut-out and norm-exponent arguments (lines 2439–2917).** The inner-collar lower estimate follows from concavity of the nth root of inner-parallel volume; the upper estimate follows from coarea and surface-area monotonicity. The shell argument in Theorem `thm:cutout` establishes an upper box-dimension lower bound along a sequence of scales, which is the appropriate conclusion. The restriction on s' in the proof can be met while taking s up to s_N. Proposition `prop:main` correctly compares the integral of an initial segment of a decreasing sequence of logarithmic singular-value ratios with its full average. The fastness-to-run-bound equivalence, submultiplicativity argument, Fekete bound, strict pressure monotonicity, and critical divergence are consistent. The piecewise-constant certificate respects shared-cell boundaries through the minimum convention.

**Counting and higher dimensions (lines 2919–3290).** I checked the permutation bookkeeping linking Baragar's tree heights with the soul norms, and the reversal of products in the dictionary for the GMR count. At the symmetric starting point, the factor d/(d+1) in the conversion to the unrestricted count is justified by permutation symmetry. The four-dimensional polynomial-certificate argument separates a finite sum from a positive tail and then controls the change in the exponent. As a numerical arithmetic check, direct summation of the displayed series S through 100,000 terms gives about 0.7045343934, consistent with the quoted 0.7046 bound; this check is not a rigorous independent certificate of its tail. The perturbation comparison is 0.0008416447 < 0.0008602389. The three-dimensional diameter induction, octahedral-volume decomposition, and surface-area bound are coherent. The paper correctly declines to apply the lower cut-out theorem when zone boundaries are not contained in the residual gasket.

**Entropy and diffusion (lines 3292–3701).** I checked the run coding, variation bounds, pressure comparison, finite roof expectation, and the entropy inequality. Kingman's theorem is applied to the two subadditive logarithmic norm sequences, giving the necessary mean convergence before the entropy/singular-value comparison. Dividing that comparison by the number of runs gives the displayed bound with the correct inequality direction; the final numerical quotient is approximately 0.7765416402, below 0.7766. The first-return coding explicitly accommodates continuation of the last run of the fixed path. The proof that the return alphabet gives a full shift and the induced Gibbs comparison address the possible overlaps of the marker word. Integrability follows from Kac's identity. The assertions about the entropy of a noninvertible suspension are expressly made conditional in Remark `rem:maxentflow` and are not used to prove the diffusion inequality. I found no substantive internal error in this chain of reasoning, subject to the external-input limitations above.

## Minor presentation issues

1. **Line 3162, definition preceding Lemma `lem:oct`:** `Z_I=A_I(\Oct)=\psi_I(\Oct)` identifies an unnormalized linear image with a normalized projective image. Literally, the two lie in different affine hyperplanes. The intended definition is unambiguously `Z_I=\psi_I(\Oct)` (or the projectivization of the linear image), as is used throughout the proof. This is a harmless notation slip and does not affect a claimed conclusion.

2. **Line 1051, Theorem `thm: dimH`:** the phrase “correct to four decimal places” should distinguish truncation from rounding. The proved interval fixes the first four digits after the decimal point as 6853, but by itself does not determine rounding to four decimal places, since it crosses 1.68535. The displayed interval and the expansion with an ellipsis are unaffected; this is a wording issue, not an error in the dimension bound.

No other issue rose to the level of a substantive mathematical error or an unjustified inference in my review. Acceptance remains subject to the stated limits of a text-only review of a paper containing substantial computer-assisted proofs.
