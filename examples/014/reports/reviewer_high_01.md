ACCEPT

I found no substantive mathematical error in my review of the supplied current paper, *The Levitt–Yoccoz gasket: dynamics, Hausdorff dimension and norm exponent*. This verdict concerns the mathematical arguments and computational certification method presented in this paper; it is not formal verification or independent reproduction of the reported large computations.

## Coverage and independence

I read the entire supplied `paper.tex`, lines 1–4102, including the introductory claims, every proof, the computational sections, and both the embedded bibliography and final bibliography. I used bounded line-numbered reads; I reread the portion of one longer output that had been truncated. I did not read another version, another workspace document, an agent report, or the external computational deposit, and did not consult other agents. I did not use a formal proof assistant. I reviewed the TeX directly rather than visually inspecting the PDF. Thus the figures were assessed through their captions and accompanying definitions, not by an independent graphical inspection.

## Main mathematical checks

1. **Zones, geometry, and dynamics (lines 1237–1651).** I checked the frame recursion, disjointness/decomposition of the cylinders and zones, the induction controlling cross products and inverse norms, the area formula, and the diameter estimates in Lemma `lem:geom`. In particular, the sum of the frame norms is twice the matrix norm, which is essential to the stated comparisons. The derivative computation and strict distance decrease in Proposition `lem: Dpsi` are consistent. The shrinking-cylinder proof of Theorem `cor:singlenode` correctly gives uniform Hausdorff convergence on the hyperspace, and the last-n-steps argument excludes chain recurrence outside the gasket. The accumulation and freeness conclusions follow from disjoint open zones and shrinking cylinders.

2. **Affinity exponent and cut-out dimension (lines 1653–1926).** I checked the determinant-based lower bound and inverse-norm upper bound for the middle singular value, including the two sharpness families. The exterior-product proof of Lemma `lem:diamZ`, the derivative comparison in Lemma `lem:DI`, and the resulting weight comparison in Proposition `prop:phiD` are compatible. The perimeter/area series and inner-collar argument correctly yield the upper box-dimension bound. Consequently the equality of box and Hausdorff dimension follows from the explicitly stated external equality with the affinity exponent.

3. **Runs, directions, and the affinity certificates (lines 1928–2437).** I checked the ordering of runs, the exact norm cocycle, the normalized derivative cocycle, and the identity decomposing the series into transfer-operator iterates. The positivity argument in Proposition `cor:certA` gives the stated directions of both bounds. The symmetry reduction preserves the necessary inequalities. The interpolation lemma and Proposition `prop:P1` provide pointwise, not merely nodal, certificates: the signs of the Hessian errors and the use of nonnegative interpolation weights are correct. The convex decreasing tail sums have their integral bounds in the correct direction. The reported choices of the binary64 parameters imply the strict decimal endpoints, provided the stated certificates hold.

4. **General cut-out and norm-exponent arguments (lines 2439–2917).** I checked the convex-body collar estimate, the division of the upper-bound series at the stated scale, and the shell argument for the lower upper-box-dimension bound. The singular-value comparison in Proposition `prop:main` correctly includes the saturation at ambient dimension. In the fast-semigroup argument, the passage from fastness to bounds across distinct runs, the submultiplicative sequence used in Fekete's lemma, and the strict pressure decrease are coherent. The sharp junction estimate and the contraction estimate justify the later symbolic construction. The piecewise-constant norm certificates correctly handle points shared by multiple closed cells by taking the minimum cell value.

5. **Counting and higher dimensions (lines 2919–3290).** I checked the permutation bookkeeping connecting the two tree constructions, the reversal in the transposed counting dictionary, and the symmetry factor in the deduction of the strong soul-counting asymptotic. The Sierpiński calculation supplies an explicit lattice contrast. For the four- and five-coordinate matrix systems, the projective coordinates, cylinder bounds, norm cocycles, and symmetry reductions are consistent. The octahedral volume decomposition and diameter/surface-area estimates have the stated powers. The paper correctly declines to apply the lower cut-out theorem to the three-dimensional gasket because zone boundaries are not wholly in the gasket. The face embedding gives the stated lower dimension bound.

6. **Invariant measure and diffusion (lines 3292–3701).** I checked the symbolic coding, uniform variation bounds for the roof, and comparison of pressure with the run series. Given the quoted thermodynamic theorems, the Gibbs measure has finite roof integral and the entropy identity used in the Lyapunov argument. Kingman's theorem is applied to the two appropriate exterior-power norms; the entropy-versus-singular-value calculation yields the displayed diffusion bound with the correct direction. I also checked the first-return-word construction: the fixed prefix of length one less than the defining cylinder length is what permits arbitrary concatenation of return words, including overlapping returns. The induced Gibbs estimate follows from bounded distortion and the quasi-Bernoulli bound; it gives summability at the critical parameter directly. Kac's formula and the comparison of cocycle singular values then give the claimed rescaling under induction. The unproved suspension-flow assertions in Remark `rem:maxentflow` are expressly separated from the theorem on diffusion and the base-map entropy statement, so they are not a missing step in those conclusions.

## Minor wording issue

**Theorem `thm: dimH`, line 1051:** “correct to four decimal places” should be read as specifying the first four digits after truncation. The proved interval (1.6853<D<1.6854) by itself does not determine the value rounded to four decimal places: that would additionally require knowing on which side of (1.68535) the value lies. The later numerical estimate suggests the lower side but is explicitly non-rigorous. This is a harmless precision/wording issue, not a failure of the rigorous interval or any dependent theorem. “The first four digits after the decimal point are 6853” would avoid the ambiguity.
