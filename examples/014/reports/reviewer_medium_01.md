ACCEPT

I found no substantive mathematical error in my review of the supplied current paper. This is a mathematical reading and consistency review, not formal verification or an independent reproduction of its computer-assisted certificates.

Coverage and method

I read the complete supplied paper.tex, lines 1–4102, including the embedded bibliography, all mathematical sections, computational descriptions, and final bibliography. I used bounded, line-numbered reads and reread the portion obscured by one output truncation. I did not consult another version, external references, other workspace files, other reviewers, or a proof assistant. I reviewed the TeX source rather than independently inspecting the rendered PDF. The figures were not needed for the arguments checked below.

The review covered the following chains of reasoning:

* Zones, frames, and dynamics (lines 1237–1651). The recursive decomposition, the frame and row cross-product estimates, the area formula, and uniform cylinder shrinking support the distinctness of souls, freeness, and the chain-recurrence conclusion. In particular, the exclusion of chain-recurrent points outside the gasket uses a fixed number of final steps of an arbitrary chain, and repetition excludes short return chains; it does not assume uniform control of arbitrarily long errors.
* Singular values and dimension (lines 1653–1926). I checked the use of determinant one, the middle-singular-value bounds, the comparison of zone diameter with sigma_2/sigma_1, the derivative comparison at the central point, and the collar estimate yielding upper box dimension at most the affinity exponent. The lower derivative comparison correctly uses the projection onto T of a right singular vector of the inverse transpose. The equality of dimensions then uses the explicitly stated external dimension theorem.
* Runs, direction transport, and certification (lines 1928–2437). The run norm and derivative factors telescope in the stated order. The transfer operator adds runs with different adjacent indices. Its weights are uniformly summable for 0 < kappa < 1. The positive-test-function criteria and the symmetry reduction have the required directions of implication. I checked the interpolation-error argument, including the mixed second-moment bound for triangular prisms, and the treatment of piecewise-polynomial kinks by increment bounds. The summation inequalities for decreasing convex tails are in the correct direction.
* General cut-out and norm results (lines 2439–2917). The inner-collar lower estimate follows from concavity of the nth root of the eroded volume; it does not require spherical zones. The upper and lower cut-out arguments use respectively all scales and a sequence of sufficiently populated shells, consistent with their conclusions about upper box dimension. The general affinity/norm comparison includes the necessary minimum with the ambient dimension. The run-pressure proof uses submultiplicativity after a bounded correction and the finite-number-of-small-words hypothesis to obtain strict decrease. The piecewise-constant certificates correctly handle points belonging to several closed cells by taking the smallest cell value.
* Arithmetic dictionary and higher dimensions (lines 2919–3290). I checked the permutation bookkeeping in the trees and the transposition/reversal in the counting dictionary, as well as the symmetry factor in the passage from restricted words to all souls. The higher-dimensional zone decomposition distinguishes faces included in zones from faces in the gasket. In particular, the paper does not incorrectly apply the lower cut-out bound to the three-dimensional gasket. The diameter induction in Lemma geom3 and the octahedral volume decomposition are consistent with the stated estimates.
* Symbolic and ergodic arguments (lines 3292–3701). The roof is bounded below and has summable variations, and its integrability follows from the Gibbs cylinder estimate. The entropy/singular-value inequality has the correct sign and yields the displayed diffusion bound. I examined the first-return-word construction, including overlapping occurrences of the defining path: the fixed prefix of length m−1 supplies precisely the extra symbols needed to determine whether an earlier return occurs. The induced Gibbs estimate and the pressure-zero argument consequently have the stated dependence on the original Gibbs measure. The text explicitly separates the unproved suspension-flow assertions from the proved entropy-per-unit-time assertion for the base map.

Minor points, not substantive errors

1. Lines 2403–2413, Remark labelled rem:P0: after defining lower and upper expressions using a minimum or maximum of the entries H over an image stencil, the text calls these “two non-negative matrices.” As written, those expressions are generally nonlinear in H. They are monotone, positively homogeneous maps; after fixing a minimizing or maximizing choice for each stencil one can represent that particular evaluation by a non-negative matrix. This is a terminology error, not a failure of the certificate: the inequalities for the actual bounded test function are what Proposition cor:certA uses. Lines 2884–2914 correctly describe the analogous norm computation as nonlinear power iteration. No claimed numerical bound is invalidated by this wording alone.

2. Lines 1636–1640, Remark labelled rem:RP2: “global attractor” on the larger spaces must use attraction in the usual one-sided set-distance sense. Invariance of the entire compact phase space suffices for that statement, but it would not imply convergence in symmetric Hausdorff distance from every compact initial set. For example an initial compact subset of T stays in T and cannot converge in Hausdorff distance to the larger triangle Delta. The paper separately states the stronger Hausdorff convergence result on T. I regard clarification of this convention as advisable, rather than a counterexample to its theorem on T.

Limitations relevant to the numerical conclusions

The supplied paper describes, but does not contain, the large test-function files, executable verifiers, and complete logs deposited elsewhere. I checked the mathematical design of the certificates and the deductions from the reported inequalities. I did not independently execute or audit those external programs or reconstruct their billions of cell checks. Thus my review does not independently validate the reported computational outcomes at lines 2373–2395, 2904–2914, 3075–3109, or 3268–3281. The explicit small polynomial test function is present, but its complete interval evaluation is still reported rather than reproduced here.

Likewise, I checked how the paper uses its stated external theorems, but did not verify their statements against the cited publications, as the review was restricted to this paper. The identifications with the cited Novikov, arithmetic-counting, and chaotic-section results retain that external dependency. Subject to those ordinary and explicitly stated review limitations, I found the main internal arguments coherent and no substantive error requiring rejection.
