## Mathematical assessment

1. **Rate convention and quadratic analysis.** Definition `def:rate` (lines 371–383) places the supremum before the asymptotic limit, and the later proofs consistently use that convention. The finite-horizon compression and smoothing argument (lines 2253–2290) preserves initial states and approximates gradients along the finite trajectory, which is enough for the claimed equality of uniform envelopes. The submultiplicative-envelope proof (lines 2293–2311), Jury stability calculation (lines 2313–2321), price-of-extrapolation inequality and equality conditions (lines 2337–2348), and slow-root calculations (lines 2350–2383, 4093–4116, 4350–4381) have the required signs and parameter restrictions. The generating-function argument for rest initialization (lines 3213–3244) correctly rules out cancellation of nonzero characteristic modes.

2. **Flow rate and smooth witnesses.** I checked the cancellation in the Lyapunov identity `eq:identity` and its proof (lines 549–559 and 2617–2637). The positive coefficient `Delta_c` supplies state coercivity, and taking exponents below the claimed infimum handles endpoint degeneracies. The orbit gradients and values in `eq:cspiral`–`eq:cvalue`, the two directed interpolation inequalities, and the minimizer constraint (lines 2435–2511) agree with differentiation and convexity. The three-regime proof of `lem:spread` (lines 2535–2615) chooses the frequency scale before the quadrature size and controls arbitrarily long lags through the hyperbolic terms. The extension argument (lines 2514–2533, 2643–2658) converts the positive interpolation margin and Lipschitz gradient data into a finite-smoothness objective. Consequently, the flow sharpness claim does not depend on pretending that the necessary smoothness bound stays fixed as the exponent approaches theta/3.

3. **Spectral closure and realizations.** The wave calculation (lines 2731–2775) reproduces both directed lag inequalities and both minimizer inequalities, including the value constant C. Compactness and finite-constraint separation (lines 2796–2822) give the stated dual certificate. The weighted IQC and feedback argument (lines 2848–2988) uses a strictly proper stable transfer function when the comparison radius exceeds the middle-curvature root radius; its subsequent tail estimate gives a strict uniform rate, rather than merely square summability for individual trajectories. The realization and closure arguments (lines 2991–3015) then fit together without reversing the direction of the bounds. For finite attainment, the analytic limiting certificate has strictly negative endpoint values (lines 3042–3080); finite support, strict minimizer slack, inactive tails and the polynomial zero-count bound are established in that order (lines 3083–3143). The exclusion of the zero exponent from that finite-attainment conclusion is explicit and appropriate.

4. **Initialization and phase locking.** The additional scalar coordinate in `lem:startup` (lines 3146–3211) provides a uniform positive interpolation margin for the modified initial datum. For a growing wave, the proof explicitly uses strict minimizer slack to dominate the cross terms; for a non-growing wave, boundedness is sufficient. This addresses the difference between arbitrary initial states and initialization from rest. The phase-locking threshold in `lem:single` and `prop:cycles` (lines 3301–3347) follows from the residues modulo K and is compatible with the signs in the lag-one symbol.

5. **General-memory and trace arguments.** The slow normal form and sampled-flow transfer (lines 3595–3768) retain the actual member parameters before taking limits and use a uniform margin for all lags. The higher regularity assumption in the faithful-discretization theorem is used in the mixed Taylor estimate (lines 3792–3845), controlling intermediate frequency scales as well as fixed and slow frequencies. The below-wall quadratic and nonlinear constructions are separately justified (lines 3847–3888). In the trace proof (lines 3894–3957), the possible simple pole at minus one is cancelled; the paired Schwarz kernel gives the displayed normalization; and the reduced-polynomial, interior-curvature argument supplies the stronger rigidity statement without assuming endpoint strict stability.

6. **Boundary layer, corner and finite condition numbers.** I checked the relation between the lag-one and mixed symbols, the coefficient expansions, and their use in the upper bounds (lines 3978–4264). The half-step spread lemma (lines 3459–3581) controls both signs of the lag constraints and obtains finite support only after securing strict margins and an inactive tail. Its application in `lem:spreadwit` (lines 4290–4337) verifies the shrinking-band scale conditions rather than assuming that a single resonant atom gives the sharp constant. The layer proof (lines 4645–4708) uses the correct scales xi of order epsilon^(1/2) and frequency of order epsilon^(1/4). The corner theorem expressly leaves an arithmetic gap when the continuous minimum occurs between the accessible resonant points; it does not claim an exact formula in that unresolved region. The optimal-tuning necessity proof (lines 4975–5146) treats bounded deficit, moderate shrinking-frequency dips and deep dips separately. The lag-two completion of the square and affine-interpolation argument (lines 5175–5230) support the finite-condition-number threshold.
