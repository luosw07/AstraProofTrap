                                                         EXCEPTIONAL LOCI IN LEFSCHETZ THEORY




arXiv:2106.10332v1 [math.AG] 18 Jun 2021
                                                                       SAM RASKIN AND GEOFFREY SMITH

                                                  Abstract. Let φ : X → Pn be a morphism of varieties. Given a hyperplane H in Pn ,
                                                  there is a Gysin map from the compactly supported cohomology of φ−1 (H) to that of
                                                  X. We give conditions on the degree of the cohomology under which this map is an
                                                  isomorphism for all but a low-dimensional set of hyperplanes, generalizing results due
                                                  to Skorobogatov, Benoist, and Poonen-Slavov. Our argument is based on Beilinson’s
                                                  theory of singular supports for étale sheaves.




                                                               1. Introduction and statement of results
                                             In this note, we prove a generalization of the following theorem due to Benoist and
                                           written here in a form due to [7].
                                           Theorem 1.1 ([2], Théorème 1.4). Let X ⊂ PN be a geometrically irreducible quasipro-
                                           jective variety over a field k. Define Mbad ⊆ P̌N as the locus of hyperplanes H such that
                                           XH := X ∩ H is not geometrically irreducible. Then codim Mbad ≥ dim X − 1
                                             This result is geometric, but has a cohomological reformulation: the top degree com-
                                           pactly supported cohomology groups of X and XH are isomorphic. In this note, we
                                           prove a similar result for all cohomology of sufficiently high degree on X.
                                           Theorem 1.2. Let X be a separated scheme of finite type over a separably closed field
                                           k, let φ : X → Pn be a morphism, and let Λ = Z/ℓZ for ℓ a prime power not divisible
                                           by the characteristic of k. Set r = dim X ×Pn X.
                                             Then for each c ≥ 1, there is a closed subscheme Zc ⊂ P̌n of dimension at most n − c
                                           such that for H ∈ P̌n \ Zc and q > c + r (resp. q = c + r), the Gysin map
                                                                        Hcq−2(φ−1 (H), Λ(−1)) → Hcq (X, Λ)
                                           is an isomorphism (resp. surjective).
                                             In the setting of Theorem 1.1, taking q = 2 dim X, we see that Mbad ⊂ Zdim X−1 ,
                                           which has dimension ≤ n − dim X + 1, recovering the assertion of loc. cit.

1.1. Past results. Theorem 1.2 generalizes a number of results beyond Theorem 1.1.
Poonen-Slavov [7] establish the q = 2 dim X case of Theorem 1.2 under the additional
assumption that φ has equidimensional fibers. If φ is the immersion of a normal projec-
tive complex variety and c = 1, Theorem 1.2 is Corollary 7.4.1 of [5], and is proven as a
special case of the paper’s Lefschetz hyperplane theorem for intersection homology.
   And if φ is the closed immersion of a smooth projective variety, this result is known;
by [9, Theorem 2.1], there exists an isomorphism Hcq−2 (φ−1 (H), Λ(−1)) → Hcq (X, Λ) so
long as q ≥ n + s + 3, where s is the dimension of the singular locus of φ−1 (H). So the
locus where there exists no isomorphism Hcq−2 (φ−1 (H), Λ(−1)) → Hcq (X, Λ) is contained
in the locus of hyperplanes such that φ−1 (H) is singular in dimension at least q − n − 2.
This locus in turn has codimension at least q − n − 1.

                    2. The Gysin map for bounded complexes
2.1. Notation and the basic setup. Let k be a separably closed field, let ℓ be a prime
power not divisible by the characteristic of k, and set Λ = Z/ℓZ. For the remainder
of this paper, sheaves will be constructible étale sheaves of Λ-modules, and for any
variety V we will use D(V ) to denote the bounded derived category of constructible
sheaves of Λ-modules on V . Given a k-point H ∈ P̌n , let i : H → Pn denote the
corresponding inclusion, and let j : Pn \ H → Pn be the inclusion of the complement.
For the remainder of the paper, all functors will be derived; for instance, we will use
j∗ to denote the derived pushforward associated to j. Finally, we will use the notation
Hq (F ) to denote the hypercohomology of F ∈ D(V ) in degree q.
   On the level of sheaves, the map in Theorem 1.2 is induced by applying RΓ to a
composition of two arrows in D(Pn ). Given a bounded complex of sheaves F on Pn , the
two maps we will use are:
      • The counit map i! i! F → F , which appears in the localization triangle

2.2. The counit map. Of the two maps above, the counit map is the easier one to
understand, as its cone is j∗ j ∗ F .
Lemma 2.1. Let F ∈ D(Pn ). Fix an integer r. Suppose that for all p, the sheaf
Hp (F ) has support of dimension at most r − p. Then the sheaf Hp (j∗ j ∗ F ) has support
of dimension at most r − p and we have Hq (Pn , j∗ j ∗ F ) = 0 for q > r.
Proof. Let F and r satisfy the hypotheses of the lemma. For each p, the support of
Hp (j ∗ F ) ∼
            = j ∗ Hp (F ) has dimension at most r − p. Since Pn \ H is affine, we then have
that affine vanishing [6, Theorem VI.7.3] implies that Rs j∗ (Hp (j ∗ F )) is supported in
dimension at most r − p − s and H s (Hp (j ∗ F )) = 0 if s + p > r. The results follow by
applying a spectral sequence to the filtered complex τ ≥p F .                            
Remark. The hypothesis of the lemma is equivalent to asking that F sit in perverse
degrees ≤ r. From this perspective, the claim follows from right exactness of affine
pushforwards with respect to the perverse t-structure.
2.3. The Gysin map. The following result is the most nonstandard ingredient of the
proof of Theorem 1.2.
Theorem 2.2. Let F be an object of D(Pn ), let Φ ⊂ Pn × P̌n be the universal hyperplane,
and let π1 , π2 denote the projections. Then there is a closed subscheme Z ⊂ Φ of
dimension ≤ n − 1 such that for H ∈ P̌n , the cone of the Gysin map
                                   i∗ F (−1)[−2] → i! F ,
associated to i : H → Pn is supported on π1 (Z ∩ π2−1 (H)).
  In the proof of Theorem 1.2, we will use the following corollary of this theorem.
Corollary 2.3. With F as in Theorem 2.2, for any positive integer c there is a closed
subscheme Zc ⊂ P̌n of dimension at most n − c such that for any H ∈ P̌n \ Zc the cone
of the Gysin map associated to i : H → Pn is supported on a subscheme of dimension at
most c − 2.
Proof. Take Z as in Theorem 2.2. Let Zc ⊂ P̌n be the closed subscheme where the
fibers of the projection Z → P̌n have dimension ≥ c − 1. As dim Z = n − 1, we have
dim Zc ≤ n − c, and it satisfies the conclusion by definition of Z.             

  The theory of singular support for étale sheaves was developed by Beilinson [1] and
Saito [8]. We use it in the following form.
Theorem 2.4 (Theorem 1.3, [1]). Let X be a smooth variety of dimension n and let F
be a bounded constructible complex on it.
   Then there exists a closed, conical subscheme SS(F ) ⊂ T ∗ X of dimension n such that
for every pair h : U → X smooth and f : U → Y a morphism to a smooth variety Y
such that df (T ∗ Y ) ∩ dh(SS(F )) is contained in the zero section U ⊂ T ∗ U, the map f is
locally acyclic with respect to h∗ (F ) (in the sense of [3], 2.12).
Remark. The most serious part of the theorem is the calculation of the dimension of
SS(F ).

                              3. Proof of Theorem 1.2
   In this section, we continue to use notation from the previous section.
   Let φ : X → Pn be a morphism of separated schemes of finite type over k. In this
section we establish Theorem 1.2 for the map φ. Let ΛX denote the constant sheaf on
X with fiber Λ.
   We first note that Rp φ! ΛX is supported at points over which φ has fiber dimension at
least p2 , because the stalk of Rp φ! ΛX at a point x is just Hcp (φ−1 (X), Λ) by [10, Lemma
0F7L]. As a consequence, if X ×Pn X has dimension r, we have that Hp (φ! ΛX ) has
support in dimension at most r − p. So by Lemma 2.1 we have that for any inclusion
of a hyperplane i : H → Pn , the hypercohomology groups Hq (Pn , j∗ j ∗ φ! ΛX ) vanish for
q > r. So we have that the counit map Hq (H, i! φ! ΛX ) → Hcq (X, Λ) is an isomorphism if
q ≥ r + 2 and is a surjection if q = r + 1.
   We now show that the Gysin map induces an isomorphism or surjection on cohomol-
ogy. Fixing c ≥ 1, let Zc be the exceptional set in Corollary 2.3 relative to the complex
of sheaves φ! ΛX . Fix some k-point H ∈ P̌n \ Zc , and let Q ∈ D(H) denote the cone of
the morphism
                                i∗ φ! ΛX (−1)[−2] → i! φ! ΛX .
By Corollary 2.3, Q is supported on a closed subscheme B of dimension at most c − 2.
Moreover, Hp (j∗ j ∗ φ! ΛX ) has support of dimension at most min(r − p, n) by Lemma 2.1.
Since we also have that Rp φ! ΛX is supported in dimension min(r−p, n), the distinguished
triangle i! i! φ! ΛX → φ! ΛX → j∗ j ∗ φ! ΛX → gives the bound on the dimension of the
support of Hp (i! i! φ! ΛX ),

                      dim supp(Hp (i! i! φ! ΛX )) ≤ min(r − p + 1, n).
  Likewise, Hp (i∗ φ∗ ΛX (−1)[−2]) is supported on a set of dimension at most min(r −
p + 2, n − 1). From these two observations and the defining triangle for Q, we see
that Hp (Q) is supported on a subscheme of dimension at most min(r − p + 1, c − 2).
Therefore, Hq−p (H, Hp (Q)) = 0 for q − p > 2 min(r − p + 1, c − 2). Observe that if
q − p ≤ 2 min(r − p + 1, c − 2), then 2(q − p) ≤ 2(r − p + 1) + 2(c − 2), so q ≤ r + c − 1.
Therefore, whenever q > r + c − 1, Hq−p (H, Hp (Q)) = 0. Applying the Grothendieck
spectral sequence, we find that Hq (H, Q) = 0 for q > r + c − 1.

    From this, we conclude that the Gysin map
             H q−2 (φ−1 (H), Λ(−1)) ∼
                 c                  = Hq−2 (H, i∗ φ! ΛX (−1)) → Hq (H, i! φ! ΛX )
is an isomorphism for q > r + c and a surjection if q = r + c. Combining this with the
counit map above, and noting c ≥ 1, we have that the map on cohomology
                                Hcq−2(φ−1 (H), Λ(−1)) → Hcq (X, Λ)
is an isomorphism for q > r + c and a surjection if q = r + c, proving Theorem 1.2 for
X.
Remark. In the above setting, note that φ! (ΛX ) ∈ D(Pn ) is in perverse degrees ≤ r.
The argument shows that, suitably understood, the conclusion of Theorem 1.2 holds for
any F ∈ D(Pn ) a bounded complex of constructible sheaves in perverse degrees ≤ r.
