ACCEPT

I found no substantive mathematical error in the principal results after reviewing the complete supplied paper. This is a mathematical reading and spot-checking review, not formal verification.

Coverage and method

I read the complete standalone TeX source, lines 1–5440, including the introduction, all proofs and examples, and bibliography. I used bounded excerpts; the small omitted segment in one truncated tool response was subsequently read separately. I did not consult another version, external sources, other workspace inputs, or other reviewers. I did not use a formal proof assistant. I reviewed the source rather than the rendered PDF. In particular, I followed the exterior-system equivalence, the reduction and integration of the Cauchy problem, the lapse-independent uniqueness argument, the supersymmetric specialization and constraint propagation, and the full leafwise averaging and compact-rigidity argument. Long component curvature calculations were checked structurally and in special cases rather than recomputed independently in every component. Results expressly imported from the bibliography were not independently verified against those sources.

Main mathematical assessment

1. Theorem labelled thm:wellposedFinalSkew (lines 2012–2146), together with lemma:uniqueevol (2259–2350): the integration is consistent. The metric evolves by pullback, the normalized spatial current is time-independent, and the remaining screen phase has the stated sign. The spatial constraints force d r = f e_u and f r = 0; consequently d(r^2)=0, and connectedness gives constant r, including the zero branch. This does not illicitly divide by r. The final scalar constraint fixes the remaining normal torsion component. The null-current straightening argument fixes the metric, current and spinor square from their initial restrictions and proves uniqueness without assuming the lapse is predetermined. Allowing point-dependent flow intervals and restricting to the domain of dependence addresses the noncompact incomplete-flow issue.

2. Corollaries cor:initialdataskew and cor:compactconstraintbranches (2628–2790): the coframe constraints translate correctly into the single bracket identity [e_l^#,e_n^#]=-r exp(f)e_u^#. The contact construction uses the bracket to select the transverse vector, rather than incorrectly identifying it with the Reeb vector. The torus-mapping-torus construction has the appropriate endpoint transformation B(1)=A^{-1}. The global classification also relies on the expressly cited classification theorem for locally free R^2 actions.

3. Theorem thm:wellposedFinalSusy (3857–3958): the dilatino condition indeed sets r=0. The proposed curving is compatible with the fixed connective structure because its increment is a basic two-form. The identity d(exp(-f)c)=L_X H_b supplies the otherwise necessary flux-closure condition. The uniqueness claim is appropriately made for the metric, dilaton, curvature, and spinor, not for all gerbe connective data merely from their curvature.

4. Theorems thm:compatibilityflows and thm:susyflowcompatibility (3657–3773 and 4003–4473): the claimed constraint identities and reduced defects have consistent tensor types and normalizations. The propagation argument retains the Einstein defect while deriving the Hamiltonian propagation equation; it does not assume the desired Einstein evolution equation. The characteristic field is null and its maximal curves meet the Cauchy surface, which is essential for global propagation and is explicitly addressed. The rotating-frame example at 5235–5279 independently checks the nonzero scalar defect and its coefficient.

5. Lemma lemma:leafwiseaveraging (4547–4768): the proof works in the ambient finite-measure L^2 space and does not presume compact leaves or a smooth leafwise Hodge decomposition. Projection onto the closure of gradients, commuting unitary-flow regularization, and the integrated flat Weitzenbock identity establish that the residual pair has invariant coefficients. Integration by parts and L^2 continuity then justify the weighted wedge-pairing identity. The smoothing is only along the leaves, but that is sufficient for the identity actually applied.

6. Theorem thm:compactrigidity (4775–5229): the Maxwell integral has the required positive sign and forces the transverse dilaton component to vanish. The subsequent determinant averaging applies to leafwise closed rows, not arbitrary matrix fields. The positive basic weight is constructed in both the compact-leaf and dense-leaf cases. It yields the sum of squares |Pi(A)|^2+Q^2/2, not an unsupported positivity claim for det(A). The second use of the averaging lemma is valid because the curvature matrix has closed rows, is symmetric and trace-free, and has zero projection. Thus its determinant is -|C|^2/2 and its vanishing follows. The final argument correctly allows a nonzero constant multiple of the null current for circle-valued dilaton curvature; exact dilaton curvature forces that multiple to vanish.

Expository omissions and harmless corrections

- Corollary cor:timeindependentdevelopment, item (3), lines 2441 and 2537–2574: the proof explains completeness of X and maximality/universality, but does not explicitly establish global hyperbolicity of the full time-independent product before discussing maximality. This is a short, valid missing argument, rather than an obstruction to the claim. Write q=exp(2f)h and a causal curve as (t,p(t)). Since g=h-2 exp(-f)dt e_u, causality gives |p'|_h^2 <= 2 exp(-f)e_u(p') <= 2 exp(-f)|p'|_h and hence |p'|_q <= 2. Also g^{-1}(dt,dt)=-exp(2f)<0. Completeness of q prevents a causal curve with bounded t from escaping; it has an endpoint and extends. Every inextendible causal curve therefore crosses each t-slice exactly once. This supplies the global hyperbolicity used in the later corollaries.

- Paragraph at line 1251: “preserves a conformal transformation of every representative” should say an appropriate gauge/null-rotation transform, not conformal transformation. With exact kappa and rho, equation (eq:kapparhogauge) produces a representative with kappa=rho=0. A conformal rescaling alone does not generally do this. For example, a nonconstant null rotation of a constant Minkowski coframe cannot be made parallel by a common scalar rescaling. The intended trivial-holonomy conclusion is nevertheless correct from the displayed gauge law. I regard this as a terminology slip in the explanatory paragraph, not a substantive failure of the curvature or holonomy conclusions.

- Line 1162 has extraneous exterior-derivative symbols in the first displayed expression for L_u g. The expression should involve (i_u du) odot v + u odot (i_u dv), followed by the l and n terms, rather than products of two exterior derivatives. The next lines have the intended tensor degrees and the theorem also follows directly from uniqueness of a metric connection with prescribed torsion. This is a display typo.

- Lines 1201, 3083, and 5329 use respectively kappa where the R^2-valued o is intended, an undefined beta_t where varphi_{phi_t} is intended, and T where t is intended. These are local notation slips with no effect on the arguments.

- The wording of line 4777 reverses the most precise proportionality statement. The proof establishes varphi=c u, permitting c=0; it is not always possible to write the nowhere-zero u as a real multiple of varphi. The abstract and proof make the intended direction unambiguous, and the zero-dilaton examples confirm it.

Limitations

My acceptance concerns the mathematical arguments at the level described above. It does not certify every long expanded curvature component, the exact hypotheses of every external theorem cited, the typography of the PDF, or analytic continuous-dependence estimates in a specified function-space topology. The paper gives explicit evolution and uniqueness formulas; its use of “well-posedness” is primarily in that geometric smooth-germ sense.
