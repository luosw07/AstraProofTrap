ACCEPT

I found no substantive mathematical error in the principal results of this paper. This is an independent mathematical review, not formal verification. Several local corrections and a short omitted argument are described below; none invalidates a claimed main conclusion.

Coverage and limitations

I read the complete supplied paper.tex, lines 1–5440, including all arguments, examples, and the bibliography. The supplied paper was my only mathematical input. I did not consult other versions, other reports, external references, or online sources, and did not use a proof assistant. I reviewed the TeX rather than independently checking the rendered PDF. Results explicitly invoked from the literature were assessed as stated in the paper, without inspecting the cited works. I checked the structural and sign consistency of the displayed calculations, but did not independently recompute every component in the lengthy curvature expansions.

In particular, my review covered the spinor/isotropic-parallelism correspondence and torsion Cartan equations; the globally hyperbolic reduction; the normalized transport construction, constancy obstruction and uniqueness via null-current flow; the contact and integrable constraint branches; the bundle-gerbe reduction; the supersymmetry and bosonic constraint systems and their compatibility; and the leafwise averaging and compact rigidity arguments.

Principal checks

1. In Theorem \ref{thm:wellposedFinalSkew} (lines 2012–2146), the normalized metric evolution makes X_t independent of t. The transverse torsion difference is transported, and the rotated complex screen form gives dr wedge e_u = 0. Combining this with the contact coefficient gives r dr = 0; connectedness then makes r constant, including the identically zero branch. The remaining scalar constraint fixes the transported normal torsion. This supplies both the necessity and sufficiency of the additional obstruction, rather than assuming its propagation.

2. Lemma \ref{lemma:uniqueevol} (lines 2259–2350) supplies a geometric uniqueness argument independent of the lapse. Pullback by the null-current flow fixes the full metric, not merely its restriction to the initial slice, because the current is Killing and its initial mixed products are known. The current and metric determine the rotation rate, and the transport equation determines the spinor square. Torsion is then fixed by the complete isotropic parallelism.

3. The two constraint branches are consistent with the bracket condition [e_l^sharp,e_n^sharp] = -r exp(f)e_u^sharp. The contact converse correctly constructs the complementary vector field from the bracket, instead of assuming an arbitrary complement works. The mapping-torus construction in the integrable converse has the correct endpoint gluing condition.

4. The supersymmetric specialization has r = 0, and the gerbe-curvature construction preserves the spatial curvature through d(exp(-f)c) = L_X H_b. The uniqueness statement in Theorem \ref{thm:wellposedFinalSusy} is appropriately restricted to the metric, dilaton, curvature, and spinor; it does not incorrectly assert uniqueness of all flat gerbe data. The propagation argument in Theorem \ref{thm:susyflowcompatibility} uses null characteristics that meet the Cauchy surface, and retains the Einstein defect until its vanishing has been established.

5. Lemma \ref{lemma:leafwiseaveraging} (lines 4547–4768) handles the closure of exact leafwise forms rather than assuming the range is closed. The smoothing by the commuting, volume-preserving R^2 action justifies the weak Bochner argument. In Theorem \ref{thm:compactrigidity}, the analysis of the period group supplies the needed description and smoothness of the projection. The positive basic weight is constructed explicitly. The first determinant identity gives Q = 0 and Pi(A) = 0, and the second applies to a symmetric trace-free curvature matrix C; its determinant is -|C|^2/2. Thus the argument does not illegitimately infer pointwise vanishing of A from vanishing of its average, and does not require compact individual leaves.

Corrections and omissions

A. Corollary \ref{cor:timeindependentdevelopment}, item (3), proof at lines 2537–2573: the proof establishes completeness of X and maximality/universality, but omits the direct argument that the full stationary product is globally hyperbolic with the stated Cauchy surface. This is an expository gap that can be filled immediately from the stated hypothesis. Put q = exp(2f)h and a = exp(f)e_u, so |a|_q = 1 and exp(2f)g = q - dt odot a. For a causal curve parametrized by increasing t, its spatial velocity v satisfies |v|_q^2 <= 2a(v) <= 2|v|_q, hence |v|_q <= 2. If t had a finite endpoint, completeness of q would give a spatial limit and therefore an endpoint in the spacetime, allowing a causal continuation. Thus an inextendible causal curve has t-range R and meets each slice exactly once. This proves the omitted assertion and validates its later uses in the compact case. It does not require additional hypotheses.

B. Paragraph at line 1251: the assertion that zero rank-one class makes the connection preserve a “conformal transformation of every representative” is incorrect literally. What the gauge formula proves is that an appropriate null rotation of a representative is parallel. For example, start with a parallel null coframe in flat space and change l to l - f u for a nonconstant f. Its new kappa is -df and the rank-one class remains zero. No common conformal factor can make this coframe parallel: parallelity of the rescaled u forces the factor to be constant, after which the varying l is still not parallel. Replace “a conformal transformation of every representative” by “a suitable gauge transformation of any representative.” The claimed triviality of holonomy is nevertheless correct by this gauge transformation; none of the subsequent results uses the incorrect conformal wording. I regard this as a local terminology correction, not a substantive failure of the holonomy conclusion or the paper's theorems.

C. Local notation errors: line 1162 has exterior derivatives where the Lie-derivative expansion requires the one-forms u and v in the symmetric products; line 1201 uses kappa on the right while the left is the R^2-valued form o; line 3083 has an undefined beta_t where varphi_{phi_t} is intended; line 5329 uses T in the dilaton although the coordinate is t. These are identifiable from the immediately adjoining formulas and do not affect the arguments. The last sentence of Theorem \ref{thm:compactrigidity} at line 4777 should say the dilaton curvature is a constant multiple of the Dirac current, as proved at lines 5216–5222, to include zero dilaton curvature without reversing the relation.

Conclusion

The mathematical mechanisms behind the principal existence, uniqueness, compatibility, and compact-rigidity conclusions withstand this review. The corrections above should be made for precision, and the short global-hyperbolicity argument should be inserted. Acceptance here records that no substantive error was found within the stated review coverage, not that every identity or external prerequisite has been formally verified.
