ACCEPT

I found no substantive mathematical error in my review of the supplied current paper. This is a mathematical review, not a claim of formal verification.

Coverage and method

I read the complete standalone paper.tex, lines 1–5440, including the introduction, every theorem and proof, the examples, and the bibliography. I used bounded reads and reread the portion of lines 2781–2789 that was truncated in one tool response. My mathematical input was exclusively this paper. I did not consult another version, external references, other workspace material, or another reviewer. I reviewed the TeX rather than performing a separate visual review of paper.pdf. No formal proof assistant was used.

The review covered the torsion/coframe correspondence, the globally hyperbolic reduction, the explicit evolution and its obstruction, the null-current uniqueness argument, the contact and integrable branches, the gerbe and dilaton reduction, the supersymmetry constraints and propagation argument, and the compact rigidity proof. I checked the structure and signs of the main reductions and selected component identities; I did not independently expand every component of each lengthy curvature calculation. Results cited from the bibliography, especially the spinorial-square reconstruction and the classification of locally free R^2 actions, were assessed as invoked hypotheses/results and were not independently verified against their sources.

Principal checks

1. The exterior system in Theorem thm:existencenullcoframeII (lines 1131–1170) has a sound converse: the proposed connection forms are metric compatible and their alternating derivatives give the specified torsion. Uniqueness of a metric connection with prescribed torsion supplies the required full covariant equations. Thus alternation alone is not being used without a way to recover the symmetric part.

2. The main Cauchy theorem thm:wellposedFinalSkew (lines 2012–2146) derives a real obstruction, rather than silently assuming preservation of all spatial constraints. The transport solution gives d r = f e_u, while differentiating yields f r = 0. Therefore d(r^2)=0 and connectedness indeed implies r is constant, including the r=0 case. The residual scalar equation then determines the remaining torsion component. The rotated pullback formula preserves the metric and supplies the claimed local solution.

3. The proof of lemma:uniqueevol (lines 2259–2350) compares developments using their null Killing currents. Their pullback metrics and null forms agree directly from their initial values. The screen two-forms obey the same linear ordinary differential equation, so the subsequent identification of the isotropic parallelisms and torsion is justified. This does not require an a priori common lapse.

4. The contact-branch construction in Proposition prop:geometryconstraintbranches (lines 2670–2730) correctly chooses the transverse vector as [X,Y]/e_u([X,Y]); it does not incorrectly identify this vector with the Reeb field. The distinction is explicitly addressed later. In the integrable branch the commuting frame gives flat translation leaves, and the torus-bundle converse constructs globally descending commuting fields even when the monodromy does not admit a real logarithm.

5. In Theorem thm:wellposedFinalSusy (lines 3857–3958), the curving construction respects the initial connective structure. The calculation d(e^{-f} c)=L_X H_b proves that the spatial curving curvature evolves by pullback, and so checks the closure/curvature compatibility that would otherwise be an extra condition. The uniqueness assertion is correctly restricted to metric, dilaton, flux and spinor; it does not claim unjustified uniqueness of the entire gerbe connection from curvature data alone.

6. In Theorem thm:susyflowcompatibility (lines 4003–4473), the proof retains the Einstein defect while deriving constraint propagation. Consequently it is not assuming the Einstein evolution equation in order to prove that equation. The transport characteristics are null and meet the initial Cauchy surface; this is the needed global ingredient beyond merely writing homogeneous transport equations.

7. Lemma lemma:leafwiseaveraging (lines 4547–4768) does not require closed leaves or a closed range for the leafwise differential. It projects onto the closure of exact pairs. The remainder is weakly closed and coclosed; convolution by the commuting measure-preserving R^2 action makes the integration-by-parts identity legitimate. The resulting coefficients are invariant, and the final pairing passes to the limit in L^2. This addresses the dense-leaf difficulty.

8. In Theorem thm:compactrigidity (lines 4775–5229), the Maxwell integral first forces the transverse dilaton component to vanish. The determinant averaging is applied to leafwise closed rows, not to arbitrary matrices. The weight 1/a in the compact-leaf case satisfies the stated differential equation and removes the Z(P) term after integration. The resulting integral is a sum of nonnegative terms, implying Q=0 and Pi(A)=0. The curvature matrix is then shown symmetric, trace free, and to have zero projection; applying the same determinant identity to its closed rows yields its vanishing. These are distinct, justified steps; Pi(A)=0 alone is not being mistaken for A=0.

Expository corrections and limitations, not substantive objections

• Corollary cor:timeindependentdevelopment, item (3), lines 2441 and 2537–2573: the proof proceeds from completeness to maximality without explicitly proving that the full stationary product has the initial slice as a Cauchy surface. The omitted estimate is short and validates the claim. Write q=e^{2f}h and beta=e^{-f}e_u. For a causal curve parameterized by the stationary time t, g(dot gamma,dot gamma)<=0 gives |dot p|_h^2<=2e^{-f}e_u(dot p)<=2e^{-f}|dot p|_h, hence |dot p|_q<=2. Completeness of q prevents escape in a bounded time interval. A finite endpoint in t therefore gives a spacetime endpoint and an extension. Each inextendible causal curve has t-range R and meets each t-slice exactly once. In addition dt is timelike because g^{-1}(dt,dt)=-e^{2f}. This fills the missing global-hyperbolicity sentence; it does not require an additional assumption.

• The paragraph at line 1251 says that vanishing rank-one class makes the connection preserve a “conformal transformation of every representative.” The required transformation is a gauge/null rotation from equation eq:equivalencerelation, not generally a conformal rescaling. If kappa=d a and rho=d b, choose the gauge parameters a and b to make both connection forms zero. A scalar conformal rescaling would not do that in general. I regard this as an incorrect descriptive word with an explicit correction already supplied by eq:kapparhogauge; the asserted trivial-holonomy conclusion remains valid.

• The final sentence of the statement of thm:compactrigidity, line 4777, reverses the direction of proportionality if read literally: the proven conclusion is that the dilaton curvature is a real constant times the nonvanishing Dirac current, and the constant may be zero. Lines 5216–5222 state and prove this correctly, and the examples allow zero dilaton. This is a wording correction, not a failure of the rigidity argument.

• There are several transparent notation slips: R^o_{x,y} instead of R^g_{X,Y} at line 910; h rather than g in several null-coframe formulas around lines 997 and 1060–1062; excess exterior-derivative symbols in the first displayed Lie derivative at line 1162; beta_t in place of the spatial dilaton form at line 3083; and T in place of t in the last example at line 5329. The split-dilaton formula at line 3122 should use a real-valued relative lift (as the notation psi_t in line 3164 suggests), rather than treating a circle-valued phi_t as a globally real-valued function. The intrinsic Maurer–Cartan treatment at lines 3125–3138 and the later transport construction avoid that notational ambiguity. None of these slips changes the reviewed main conclusions.

Conclusion

The explicit constructions, compatibility mechanism, and compact rigidity proof withstand this review. The above corrections would improve precision, but I found no substantive error requiring rejection. The long component computations and externally cited results remain the principal limitations of the review.
